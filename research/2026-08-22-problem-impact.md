# Impact of the problem in 2026 — research with sources

**Date:** August 22, 2026. **Purpose:** to back up `PROBLEM.md`. **Method:** six parallel searches (students, teachers, knowledge loss, agent memory and AI tutors, Argentina, tools), each with 10–22 web searches and the original source read whenever possible. The figures that support the central argument were re-verified by hand against the primary source (marked **✓ primary**). What was only seen in a search snippet is marked **~ snippet**. What is secondary (news coverage of a study) is marked **≈ secondary**.

Citation convention for `PROBLEM.md`: `[A3]` = finding 3 of section A, etc.

## G. What this research does not prove (to avoid overselling)

1. **No one directly measured** that "what a student learns privately with AI never makes it back to the group or the teacher." It's inferred from A2 (88% use it / 43% report no integration in their courses / 29% say teachers are equipped), A4 (AI before course material), and B1 (the preference for unofficial, private channels). It's a reasonable inference, not a data point.
2. The causal evidence (A6, A7, A8, B8) comes from secondary school or specific courses (physics, math), not Argentine universities. What it shows consistently: **the same AI helps when designed into the course and harms when used loose.**
3. The corporate knowledge-loss figures (C1, C2) are from 2012 and 2018; the 2025 ones (C3, C4, C5) come from vendors with a commercial interest.
4. There's no large Argentine university dataset on AI use; we use DEC LATAM (A3) and Argentinos por la Educación (A14).
5. There's no study measuring loss across cohorts; B1 (same course, thousands of new questions the following year) is the most direct proxy.
