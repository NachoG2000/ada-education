# Aleph 2026 submission rules and pitch guide

**Source:** organizer documents (event outline from Hacki/Notion + the "How to Give THE Killer Pitch or Hackathon Demo" guide), pasted by Ignacio into the chat on 2026-08-22. We don't have the original URL; verified only against the pasted text, not against the source. If anything here contradicts what DoraHacks or the official Telegram says, they win.

## What constrains our submission

- **The demo video is 3 minutes long.** Verbatim: "To participate, you must submit a 3-minute demo video showcasing your project." It's the only artifact the jury is guaranteed to watch: judging is **async, entirely online, in English or Spanish (with English subtitles)**.
- **Deadline: Sunday 08/23, 12:00 (Argentina time).** Judging 13:00–17:00, ceremony 17:00. (Matches `DECISIONS.md` §13.)
- **Open source code is mandatory**, with a link in the submission; a README with project info is recommended. All code must be written during the hackathon (commits are reviewed).
- On DoraHacks you have to **select tracks/bounties** and explain which ones you're applying to. Us: General Track only (`DECISIONS.md` §3 of the header).
- Teams of 1 to 4. If a contract were deployed you'd need to provide the address (not applicable).

## Judging criteria (all five, summarized verbatim)

1. **Technicality** — what technical challenges it tackles; whether the solution is complete and functional or there's a clear plan to finish it.
2. **Originality** — whether it's innovative, whether it tackles concrete pain points, whether it proposes a new approach to an existing problem.
3. **UI/UX/DX** — how usable and well designed the interface, the experience, and the DX are.
4. **Practicality** — how feasible it is and whether it has real-world application.
5. **Presentation** — whether the project is explained clearly in the pitch, demo, and materials.

Note: `DECISIONS.md` §16.3 cited three "unverified" criteria; this organizer text lists **five** (adding Practicality and Presentation). Presentation as an explicit criterion justifies investing in the slides and video editing; Practicality reinforces the open source pitch of "a professor can run this today on their own computer" (§15/§16).

## Structure recommended by the organizer (killer pitch guide)

For 2–5 minutes: hook (15–20s) → solution (30–40s) → traction (30–40s, "doesn't apply to most hackathon participants") → market/why now (20–30s) → tech stack (20–30s) → team (15–20s) → **live demo (60–120s)**, ideally recorded and edited. Advice we're adopting: slides with little text and strong visuals, tell a story, close strong leaving the audience excited about the future. Mistakes they list: covering too much, jargon, features instead of benefits, not rehearsing.

## What we decided from this (08/22, confirmed by Ignacio)

- The 3:00 video is structured as: **slides ~1:20 (7 slides) + recorded demo ~1:25 + closing ~10s**. The demo script from `DECISIONS.md` §11 (~3 min) gets compressed to the core steps from §15 (1–6 + terminal). Detail and script: `pitch/SCRIPT.md`.
- **Slides in English** (judging accepts Spanish, but the jury is international and the reference examples are in English). The product shown in the demo stays in Rioplatense Spanish: it's real and part of the identity.
- Pitch positioning: **broad open source** (`DECISIONS.md` §16, confirmed).
- We're skipping the "traction" section (there is none, and we're not inventing one: `PRODUCT.md`, Evidence on Hand) and compressing the team into the closing.
