## 2. Auditing the "the problem is attention and pace" claim

**Attention: real but oversold**
- Gloria Mark: time on screen before switching: 2.5 min (2004) → 75 s (2012) → median 47 s, mean 40 s (~2016+) ≈. Measures switching frequency, **not attention capacity**.
- The "8-second goldfish" claim has no peer-reviewed backing ~. Researchers describe part of the "TikTok brain" discourse as **moral panic** ≈ (2024).
- Meta-analysis (71 studies, n=98,299): correlation between heavy short-video use and worse attentional control, with **likely reverse causality** ~.
- NAEP ✓ (elementary): reading for 13-year-olds down 4 points (2020→2023), −7 points vs. the decade; PISA 2022 down 10 OECD points, device distraction −15 points controlling for socioeconomic status ≈ — but the drop is "only partially" attributable to phones.
- Banning phones: the largest study (NBER w34388, 2025) shows mixed/null academic effects ~.

**Pace (boredom from going too fast/too slow)**
- Bloom's "2 sigma" (1984) **does not replicate**: a modern meta-analysis of 96 tutoring RCTs gives ~0.37 SD ≈. The "fixed pace loses both ends" mechanism is plausible and consistent with mastery learning, but **we found no large-scale classroom RCT that proves it directly**; almost everything claiming it is edtech marketing.

**AI tutoring: the strongest causal evidence in the field**
- Harvard (Kestin et al., Scientific Reports Jun 2025): >2x learning in less time, n=194 ≈ (primary source paywalled). Nigeria (World Bank): ~0.3 SD in 6 weeks, $48/student, with a bias toward female students and higher-performing ones ≈. Already cited as [A7][A8].
- Meta-analysis reporting 0.7–0.9 SD ~: suspected of inflation (only ~20% of studies run >6 months).
- **Khanmigo "22% improvement, n=340,000": unconfirmable, traces back to a marketing blog. Never use.** Khan Academy itself said (Nov 2024) the studies were "underway"; the actual 2026 RCT gives 0.06–0.08 SD/year.
- "Personalization" failures: Summit Learning (walkouts 2018-19, no substantive improvement per Chalkbeat) ≈; AltSchool closed its schools in 2019 ≈.

**Cognitive offloading / unanchored AI**
- MIT "Your Brain on ChatGPT" (Kosmyna et al., arXiv 2506.08872): lower EEG connectivity and recall in the LLM group — **preprint, n=54, preliminary** ~. Microsoft/CMU (Feb 2025): lower self-reported critical effort among knowledge workers ≈.
- "Private chatbots fragment group learning": **no direct study exists**; it's a reasonable inference from the learning-as-social-process literature ≈. Kept as a stated inference (already framed that way in `PROBLEM.md` §10.1).

**What surveys name as problem #1**
- EDUCAUSE 2025/26: institutional trust (57%→36% over a decade) ≈. HEPI 2025: concern over misconduct accusations and incorrect outputs ≈. RAND 2025 (teachers): conduct/discipline (52-58%), pay (39-44%), administrative load. **Attention shows up, but secondary in all of them.**

## 3. Verdict (applied to Ada)

1. **"Attention crisis" is the weakest framing**: real data but a contested narrative that's partly moral panic. Don't build the pitch on it. `PROBLEM.md` §1 already discarded it ("applies to any classroom in any decade"); this audit confirms it with sources.
2. **The defensible framing remains ours**: group memory that gets lost + flight to private chatbots (documented) + teacher overload (the best-evidenced problem in the surveys). Group fragmentation via chatbots is an inference, and we should say so.
3. **AI personalization/tutoring is the rival's weapon, not ours**: the strong causal evidence (Harvard, Nigeria) is from tutors **custom-built into the course** — exactly [A6]: AI helps when anchored to the course and harms when used loose. Ada treats that as condition 7.6, not as a promise of "personalized learning."
4. Numbers banned from the pitch: the 8-second goldfish, Bloom's 2-sigma as still valid, Khanmigo's 22%, "$8B Claude Code."
